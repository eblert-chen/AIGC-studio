## Current product architecture decision

- Relay implementation direction is now `new-api`-first: adopt `QuantumNous/new-api` as the channel gateway and operations foundation, then extend it with this product's generation-specific Relay guarantees. Do not continue treating the existing Python Relay as the permanent production core.
- Preserve the existing customer Platform/Relay boundary: the browser still calls only the customer Platform, while the Platform and the internal TikTok service call the extended `new-api`-based Relay through service credentials.
- Until encrypted credential provisioning and immutable generation-route releases are fully integrated into Platform, the product owner's platform-administrator console may expose one audited, owner-only `Relay high-risk operations` launcher to the native new-api `/channels` console. It must open a separate tab from a server-owned URL, never use an iframe, never proxy arbitrary new-api APIs, and never put a new-api credential, bearer token, session, or SSO assertion in the URL. Production requires HTTPS plus a separately protected Relay management ingress and an independent new-api login or standards-based IdP SSO; the native console remains a break-glass surface rather than a customer application dependency.
- The migration must preserve or reimplement before production cutover all existing Relay safety semantics: provider-neutral `/v1/generations`, strict versioned per-mode capability declarations and revision pinning, failover-safe capability intersections, PostgreSQL-shared account admission, sticky provider-task routing, unknown-submission reconciliation with no cross-channel retry, durable async execution and token fencing, verified artifact transfer into platform-controlled storage, signed idempotent callbacks with retry/dead-letter state, provider monitoring, and append-only provider-cost events.
- Use `new-api` strengths instead of rebuilding them separately: its channel/adaptor ecosystem, OpenAI/vendor protocol compatibility, model mapping, priority/weight routing controls, multi-key/channel administration, authentication foundations, usage logs, and operator-facing channel console should become the starting point for the new Relay control plane.
- Migration is incremental and contract-first. Keep the current Python Relay available as the behavior oracle and rollback path until the extended `new-api` implementation passes contract parity, migration, failure-injection, multi-worker PostgreSQL/Redis, real-provider staging, artifact-storage, callback, and billing-reconciliation acceptance gates. Do not perform a flag-day replacement or silently weaken an existing invariant to match upstream `new-api` behavior.
- Build the requirement document's two-platform system, not a frontend-only prototype.
- `backend/platform/` is the customer management/control-plane API: companies, members, granular permissions, company entitlements, wallet ledger, billing, tasks, assets, reports, and admin operations.
- `backend/relay/` is the provider-neutral generation relay: unified generation contract, adapters, routing, account pools, durable async execution, failover, artifact transfer, and provider monitoring.
- Customer-platform roles explicitly include company owner, team lead, and operator, plus a separate platform-administrator boundary. Model, feature, agent, and external-API entitlements belong to this customer platform.
- Keep the account hierarchy simple: platform administrators, company accounts (owner, team lead, operator), and individual consumer users. Do not add a separate `super_admin` business role. The product owner's platform-administrator account receives the complete platform permission set, while future operations, finance, support, content, or channel administrators use the same platform-administrator boundary with narrower server-enforced permission assignments.
- Protect the product owner's full-permission platform-administrator account as a production security boundary. Production must use an explicit server-side owner-subject allowlist, require an IdP-signed phishing-resistant `amr` plus `auth_time`, and require recent step-up authentication for every non-read platform-admin request. Until granular platform-administrator permissions are implemented, non-owner platform administrators fail closed in production instead of inheriting full access.
- Add a TOC personal workspace alongside the existing company workspace. Individual users log in directly, hold and spend personal points, create tasks, and manage only their own works without creating a company. A natural person may also belong to one or more companies and switch explicitly between personal and company contexts; personal points, company shared wallets, retail pricing, company contract pricing, entitlements, reporting, and audit scopes must remain separated. Both contexts reuse the existing customer Platform and provider-neutral Relay rather than duplicating the generation stack.
- Creating a company atomically creates its owner account/membership. Every new non-owner member starts with exactly one primary company level (`operator` by default or `team_lead` when explicitly selected); promotion/demotion replaces that level instead of stacking both. Custom permission roles may remain additive, and platform administrators stay outside the company-role hierarchy.
- The active customer-platform permission catalog contains 16 items, each backed by an actual company-facing route check. The publishing additions are `publish.accounts.read`, `publish.accounts.manage`, `publish.jobs.read`, and `publish.jobs.manage`; they are a separate permission domain from generation tasks. Every active item must be individually configurable by the company owner for each employee. Owner/team-lead/operator and custom roles provide inherited defaults; a personal `allow` or `deny` override wins per permission, while clearing the override returns that item to `inherit`. Permission-management APIs and UI must use the complete active backend catalog rather than a fixed frontend subset.
- Full-replacement member access and permission APIs must require explicit replacement fields, reject unknown fields, and compare the caller's expected role-assignment and personal-override snapshot under a membership row lock. This snapshot does not cover role-template contents. A stale role/override editor receives `409` instead of silently overwriting another session's changes.
- `models.manage` and `tasks.manage` are retired permission codes: they must never be reused, returned by the permission catalog API, accepted for new assignments/overrides, or considered during effective-permission evaluation.
- Relay channels are explicitly classified as reverse-engineered channels, third-party API platforms, and official providers. Channel credentials, account-pool state, cooldowns, rate limits, routing, and failover belong only to the relay.
- Relay provider plugins use adapter contract version 1, explicitly declare one channel class, and are loaded through `python.module:factory` without upper-layer provider branches. Every route must publish a deterministic, non-empty, internally consistent capability for each unique `model + mode`; API and workers fail startup on an invalid declaration. Production always rejects Mock and any adapter that has not passed its own `production_ready` gate.
- Production account-pool admission is shared through PostgreSQL, not process-local counters. One stable route represents one real account; route assignment is persisted and submission-token fenced before the provider POST, fixed-window RPM and long-lived active-task capacity are enforced across workers, and disabled/cooling accounts reject only new work while existing tasks poll the original account. A proven non-creation may fail over, an unknown submission retains its route and slot for reconciliation, and local busy/RPM waits use the durable Redis delayed queue without consuming provider retry attempts.
- Relay provider monitoring runs as an independently leased durable worker. It records per-route health plus immutable provider-terminal outcomes, deduplicates trigger/recovery transitions for success-rate drops, widespread route failures, and provider-caused batch account invalidation, and delivers stable secret-free events through a signed at-least-once Webhook with token fencing, retry, and dead-letter state. PostgreSQL coordination uses the database clock. Production must enable the monitor and configure an external signed alert sink; readiness exposes monitor freshness and alert backlog without removing the API from service during a provider-only outage. Planned provider removal requires an explicit retired-provider acknowledgement and must not infer recovery merely from missing probes.
- Relay admission is bounded by the provider-neutral, failover-safe public capability contract, not by a wider private route. An unknown submission outcome can never be retried or failed over to another account/channel. New channels within the existing v1 modes and fields require only an adapter, factory/configuration, tests, and platform catalog approval; new public modes, media/output types, distributed account-pool semantics, or cost events require a versioned core change.
- The browser calls only the customer platform. The customer platform authenticates, authorizes, reserves balance, and then calls the relay. The internal TikTok system may call the relay using its own service credential.
- Social publishing is owned by the customer Platform or a dedicated Publishing Service, never by the generation Relay. Publisher connections are company-scoped destination accounts and must not share tables, credentials, rate limits, or administration surfaces with Relay provider/account-pool routes. The first production flow is durable stored artifact -> publishing account -> human approval -> scheduled queue -> official provider adapter -> result reconciliation. Development mocks must be visibly labeled and production must fail closed without a production-ready adapter; OAuth tokens stay server-side, encrypted, auditable, and revocable.
- `feature.auto_publish` gates every new external publishing side effect: connecting a new destination, creating or approving a job, retrying, and the worker's final provider POST. Removing the grant must stop all not-yet-submitted work, but it must not hide or strand historical safety operations: authorized members can still view publication history and attempts, disable connections, cancel work that has not been submitted, and reconcile `submission_unknown` after checking the provider console. Unknown submissions are never retried until an authorized reconciliation explicitly confirms that no external post exists.
- Keep money in integer minor units and use reserve -> settle/release semantics. Failed generations must not be charged.
- Company billing uses one shared wallet for every member. Every model has one catalog-level pricing mode (`per_second` or `per_item`); company grants set only that mode's positive unit price. Recharge retries reuse a stable idempotency key and must replay only when the amount and note also match. Consumption reporting must support company, employee, model, and time filters.
- Monetary database columns use 64-bit integers. Ledger rows are append-only at both ORM and database levels, including PostgreSQL `TRUNCATE` protection; a successful generation settles the reserved amount, while failure or cancellation releases it.
- A published model must have a validated, versioned generation-capability declaration. It defines supported generation modes, per-media input counts, face support, resolutions, durations, aspect ratios, output counts, prompt limits, and required resource entitlements. Empty, contradictory, misspelled, or unknown capability fields must not be publishable.
- Company model overrides may only restrict the catalog capability; they can never expand it. Company-facing model APIs return the server-computed effective capability per generation mode, and both the adaptive studio UI and task admission consume that same contract. Every task keeps the selected effective capability and catalog version as an immutable snapshot.
- The creation studio renders and validates only the selected mode's server-computed effective capability. Unsupported inputs and face controls stay hidden, per-type media counts and enumerated options are enforced, and a model or mode switch must synchronously remove or reject stale values before a task can be submitted.
- Face handling is an explicit model capability and request option, not an inferred resource name. A face-enabled request is accepted only when the selected effective capability supports it and must remain present in the Platform-to-Relay request contract. Model pricing stays on the company grant, because the same catalog model may have different customer prices.
- Platform-admin entitlements use extensible server-owned catalogs. Models remain in the model catalog; features, agents, and external-API products use the resource catalog and `ResourceKind`. The admin UI must render the complete catalog and each company's explicit enabled/disabled grants instead of hard-coding product names or assuming that missing data is enabled.
- Platform administrators need a company entitlement matrix that includes granted and ungranted items, plus catalog lifecycle controls for adding, editing, activating, and retiring features, agents, and external-API products. Retiring a catalog item must fail closed for company use without deleting historical grants.
- Suspending a company is an admission kill switch for new company requests, not an implicit cancellation of already accepted work. Existing reserved/outbox/Relay tasks must finish through the normal settle, release, reconciliation, or timeout paths so balances and upstream work cannot be stranded.
- Platform financial reporting separates recharge cashflow, successfully settled customer revenue, provider-channel cost, and gross profit. Provider costs use an append-only, idempotent cost ledger with exact channel identity, channel class, occurrence time, and external reconciliation reference; never infer provider cost from the customer price. Missing real cost records must be surfaced as incomplete reconciliation, not silently treated as a complete zero-cost period.
- Real channels, Huawei Cloud OBS, public authentication, and payments must be integrated explicitly; never represent mock implementations as production-ready.
- Relay publishes a service-authenticated, provider-neutral `/v1/models` catalog with per-mode capabilities, deterministic SHA-256 revisions, ETag revalidation, and a failover-safe intersection for shared model aliases. The customer platform may only narrow this physical capability, records the approved Relay revision, and pins it on every generation request so drift fails before any provider call.
- The canonical multimodal contract remains `/v1/generations`. It follows OpenAI-style asynchronous resource semantics (`id`/`object`, polling or callback, post-completion artifact access) but is not advertised as wire/drop-in OpenAI SDK compatibility.
- Relay submission, provider polling, artifact transfer, and callback completion use durable lease ownership with random token fencing; an expired worker may finish an external request but must never overwrite the current owner or regress a terminal state. Callback delivery remains at-least-once and claims only the item being sent.
- Generated artifacts are durable only after the Relay has verified and stored them in platform-controlled storage; provider temporary URLs must never be exposed as long-term assets. Download auditing must distinguish URL issuance or download initiation from a confirmed completed transfer. The UI may show "downloaded" only from a controlled transfer path or trusted Huawei OBS access-log/event evidence, never from a button click or signed-URL issuance alone.
- Task history, task details, artifacts, and download access are user-scoped by default: an ordinary employee may read and download only tasks they initiated. Company owners, platform administrators, or members with the company reporting permission may explicitly use company scope. Cross-scope and cross-tenant task or artifact identifiers must not reveal existence.
